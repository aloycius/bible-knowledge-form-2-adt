Excuse of inferiority

Moses’ fifth excuse to the calling of the Lord was an attitude of inferiority. Moses believed that the Lord had made a mistake in His choice, “And he said, “O Lord send, I pray, some another person” (Exodus 4:13). Moses was not the same proud prince of the Egyptian kingdom he had once been. He was now acting like a humble shepherd from the backside of the desert who had been stripped of all his pride and self-confidence. In the end, God gave Moses his brother Aaron to be his mouthpiece, not out of His wisdom, but simply to satisfy Moses’ endless requests. Moreover,

Aaron would always be a reminder to his brother that he must always depend upon

God’s help and strength in his ministry.

Excuse of unfruitfulness

Moses’ last response to God’s calling was his accusation against the Lord because of no apparent fruitfulness in what the Lord had told him to do. In Exodus 5:21-22,

Moses went to Egypt and spoke the words that the Lord had told him to speak. What

Moses had expected to happen did not happen, and he was profoundly disappointed.

Indeed, things became worse. Pharaoh did not hearken to the words of the Lord through Moses and Aaron. Pharaoh had the taskmasters beat the children of Israel because of what Moses had said. Moses was displeased because of this apparent unfruitfulness in his ministry. But at this point, the Lord was testing Moses to see the kind of reaction he would demonstrate. Moses expected results sooner than the

Lord wanted to give them. Moses was learning patience.

Synopsis of Moses’ mission

The primary mission of Moses was liberation of the sons of Israel from slavery in Egypt and lead them to the Promised Land. However, he was to face Pharaoh the king of Egypt with two-fold mission: First, to demand for the liberation of the enslaved Hebrews, in this regard, to tell Pharaoh to let the people go. Second, through him God wanted to teach Pharaoh that the gods they worshiped are not gods at all. The Lord out of the burning bush appointed Moses to fulfil these tasks.

After Pharaoh was humbled with the ten plagues, he let the people go, here the task of Moses was to lead the people on the way to the Promised Land and to become a mediator between God and His people.

Briefly, we see that throughout the journey of our discussion from the prosperity of the sons of Israel to the call of Moses, it is a reflection on God’s love and compassion, not only for the people of Israel but also for His creation at large. It is an invitation to see that God is always at work for the sustainability and salvation of his people.
