Appointment of Judges over the people

On the way to the Promised Land, Moses met his father-in-Law. When Jethro saw everything that Moses had to do, he said, “You are not doing it the right way. You will wear yourself out and these people as well. This is too much for you to do alone.

You should choose some capable men and appoint them as leaders of the people:

They must be God-fearing men who can be trusted and who cannot be bribed. Let them serve as judges for the people on a permanent basis. They can bring all the difficult cases to you, but they themselves can decide all the smaller disputes. That will make it easier for you, as they share your burden. If you do this, you will not ware yourself out, and all these people can go home with their disputes settled.”

Moses took Jethro’s advice and chose capable men among all the Israelites. He appointed them as leaders of thousands, hundreds, fifties, and tens. They served as judges for the people on a permanent basis, bringing the difficult cases to Moses but deciding the smaller disputes themselves (Exodus 18:1-27).

The above passage teaches that, in leadership there is a need to delegate power.

Delegating power is sharing the burden and makes the work successful. Also, it teaches the importance of accepting advice.

The consequences of not appreciating God’s love and compassion

Failing to appreciate God’s love and compassion, not only leads to idolatry, but degenerates living. It begins with thanklessness and indifferences and results in the lowering our standards. St. Paul in Romans 1:21 says that being unthankful to God is a corner stone of our sins. It is the way which leads to covetousness, worshiping creatures instead of God. The book of Exodus is clear on the consequences of not appreciating God’s love and compassion.

At the Sea of Reeds when the people of Israel saw the Egyptians coming after them, they forgot all that God did for them they started complaining against Moses.

Complaining to Moses was complaining to God Himself. They forgot the power of

God that He showed them in Egypt.
