The Commandment “You shall not kill”

Human life is sacred because it involves the creative work of God, and it remains forever a sole property of the creator who is transcendent and the ultimate destination to all life. The Lord God alone is the source of life; therefore, no one should take away life (cf. Exodus 20:13). This commandment aims at protecting life. The commandment forbids direct and intentional killing of human being. According to the Torah, any act of murder is considered to be gravely sinful and inhumane.

The commandment therefore forbids: Genocide - an intentional murdering of an individual with the intent to destroy, in whole or in part, a national, ethnic, racial, or religious group; Homicide – a general term referring to murdering of an individual and can happen for many reasons; Mass killing – referring to an act of killing many people in a single incidence, for example, the ethnic driven mass killing that took place in Rwanda in 1994. The Rwanda mass killing is also referred to as a genocide since it involved one ethnic group killing the other ethnic group; Infanticides – referring to an intentional killing of children/infants; Abortion – often defined as an intentional termination of unborn baby/pregnancy.

Furthermore, the commandment prohibits: Euthanasia – is the practice of intentionally ending a person’s life to relieve pain and suffering. Suicide – defined as an act of ending one’s own life voluntarily and intentionally; likewise, gender transgression is also against this commandment since it compromises human ability to bear children. It is intrinsically anti-procreation, that can lead to annihilation of human race on earth. Last but not least, is the use of contraception – basically, this involves the use of contraceptive drugs and all devices that intentionally prevent conception.

In summary, this commandment calls for the sacredness of life and demands that human life and the life of the unborn children must be protected by all means.

Moreover, the commandment calls for protection of biological health against abortion, forbids drug abuse, and drunkenness. Like the declaration of the human rights, the commandment dignifies human life and safeguards it against all forms of violence, conflicts, wars and arbitrary practices that might lead untimely death or termination of life.
