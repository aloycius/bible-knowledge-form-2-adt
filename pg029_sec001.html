Egyptians are plundered of their property

Before embarking on their exodus from Egypt, the children of Israel asked their

Egyptian neighbours for silver and gold jewellery and clothing. In accordance with

God’s will, their requests were granted. “Thus, they plundered the Egyptians”

(Exodus 12:36).

Lessons from the event of plundering the Egyptians

The act of plundering Egyptians has a lot to teach people. First, when the Israelites received these possessions was a fulfilment of God’s promise to Abraham to make him and his descendants prosperous. God is shown to be faithful to His promise.

Second, the plundering of Egyptians is one of God’s mighty works of the exodus.

Along with the plagues, it is a means by which God humbled the haughty Egyptians.

The wonders of the exodus proclaim to the world that the God of Israel is the one true God, ruler of all nations.

Third, this transfer of wealth is a judgment on Egyptians and example of God’s justice in action. By enslaving the Israelites, the pharaohs deprived them of the fruits of their labour. The pharaohs are also guilty of robbing God, the rightful master of

Israel and the one entitled to Israel’s service. The gifts given to the Israelites at their departure compensated their labour and punished the crimes of Egypt.

Ordinance on how to celebrate the Passover

Israelites were given instructions on how to celebrate the Passover in later days.

First, no foreigner shall eat of it. Every slave bought by money shall eat of it after he is circumcised. No sojourner or hired servant may eat of it. In one house shall it be eaten. You shall not carry forth any flesh outside the house. You shall not break a bone of it. All the congregation of Israel shall keep it. And when a stranger/ sojourner/native of the land wants to keep it, let he and his house be circumcised

(Exodus 12:43-50).

At night the Lord passed in the land of Egypt and killed all first born of the people and animals alike. However, He spared the first borns of the people in the house who smeared blood on the doors. The people spared included the Egyptians who followed the instruction of God.

Consecration of the first born

In Exodus 13:1-16, Moses was ordered by God to consecrate every male first born.

A sign that all male firstborns belong to God.
