The fourth plague: Swam of flies

This plague intended to prove that “Khepri” the Egyptian god who had a head of a fly is not god. For the Egyptians this was a god of creation, movement of the sun, and rebirth (Exodus 8:20-32). After seeing the intensity of the plague, Pharaoh bargained that they can go to sacrifice their God but in the land of Egypt something which was not accepted by Moses.

The fifth plague: Death of livestock

Moses once again demanded Pharaoh, “Let my people go, that they may serve me”, revealing also the next Egyptian plague to occur on the condition of continued disobedience to the request. This plague was against “Hathor” - Egyptian goddess of love and protection who had a form of a cow. The plague also was against “Apis”

– the bull god regarded as the creator god and symbol of fertility. This plague was given with an advanced warning, allowing a period of repentance to occur, which goes unheeded. “Tomorrow” the hand of the Lord would be felt upon all the cattle and livestock, of only the Egyptians.” This means that disease and pestilence would fall upon their cattle and cause them to die. This plague affected the Egyptians by creating a huge economic disaster, in areas of food, transportation, military supplies, farming, and economic goods that were produced by these livestock. Still Pharaoh’s heart remained hard and he would not listen to the Lord but remained attached to the

Egyptian gods and goddesses (Exodus 9: 1-7).

The sixth plague: Boils

Ashes turned to boils and sores. This plague was against Egyptian goddess of medicine and peace “Imhotep.” Announcement of the sixth Egyptian plague is given, for the first time, directly attacking the Egyptian people themselves. Being instructed by the Lord, Moses took ashes from the kiln-furnace of affliction (Furnace of affliction was a centre of heating, used to smelt ore, melt metal for castings, heating metal for forging, fire pottery or bricks and used to make lime. It was further used for smelting ore or for firing bricks), and threw them into the air. As the dust from the ashes blew all over Egypt, it settled on man and beast alike in the form of boils and sores. As was for the previous two plagues, the division is drawn between the

Egyptians and the children of Israel, as God gives protection to His covenant people.
