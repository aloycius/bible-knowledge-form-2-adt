Introduction

In the modern world, statistics and census are paramount for economic planning and provision of social services. The book of Numbers presents some biblical insights on the significance of statistics and census. In this chapter, you will learn the concept of statistics and census in the book of Numbers. You will also learn the objectives and significance of statistics and census for the development of the society. The competencies developed will enable you to apprehend the concept of statistics and census and appreciate their relevance for the development of the society and will enable you to apply statistics in your daily life.

Introduction to the book of Numbers

The book of Numbers is the fourth book in canonical arrangement of the Bible.

The name ‘Numbers’ is taken from the Greek word “arithmoi” given to the Greek translation of the fourth book of the Torah namely “Bemīḏbar,” which means ‘in the wildernesses. The book expresses the match of all the twelve tribes of Israel through the wilderness ending with massive invasion of Canaan from the east.

Historically, the events in the book of Numbers began in the second year after the departure from Egypt and when the Israelites camped at Mount Sinai around 1444

BCE (Numbers 1:1). The narrative ends thirty eight years later in the plains of Moab by the Jordan, opposite Jericho in 1406 BCE. Together with other things, the book provides biblical insights on the vitality of demographics and shades light to the importance of statistics and census in the contemporary society.
