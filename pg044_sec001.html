The call to fidelity

Fidelity is a state of upholding faithfulness to a person, cause, or belief; often demonstrated through continuing loyalty and unwavering support. After handing the people of Israel with the Ten Commandments, the Lord God called the Israelites to fidelity—to be faithful to His covenant. Indeed, God promised to send His angel to guide and take His chosen people to the Promised Land. He called them to listen to His voice, observe His instructions, not to be rebellious against Him. In return

God would do the following: be the enemy of Israel’s enemies and foe of their foe; leading them to the Promised Land; remove their sickness and give them full life span and bless their food and drinks; grant Israelite’s women fertility since no woman would be barren or miscarry; and give them the land from the Red Sea to the sea of Philistines; and from the desert to the river (Exodus 23: 20-33).

Ratification of the covenant

The Lord called Moses, with Aaron, Nadab, Abihu and the seventy elders of Israel.

Moses alone went closer to God while the rest were at the distance worshiping the Lord. In rectification of the covenant, the following things were done: Moses erected at the foot of the mountain an altar and twelve pillars according to the twelve tribes of Israel; They offered holocaust and sacrifice of young bulls as peace offering to God; Moses took half of the blood then put it in large bowls, and the other half he splashed on the altar and thereafter took the book of the covenant and read it before the Israelites and they answered, “All that the Lord has said, we shall listen and do.”
