Egypt with the Ten Plagues.

The first plague: Water Turned into Blood

This plague was to prove that the Egyptian god “Hopi”, god (spirit) of the Nile,

“Khnum”, the guardian of the Nile, and “Osiris” the giver of life whose bloodstream was Nile were not gods. As Aaron, the spokesman for Moses, stretched the “rod” of the Lord over the water of Egypt, Nile River, other rivers and all other sources of water in Egypt turned into blood. The water became unsuitable for drinking, all the fish died, and the rivers stank. The magicians of Pharaoh also turned water into blood, leaving Pharaoh unimpressed with this great wonder from God. For seven days the water throughout all the land of Egypt remained in this state, unsuitable for drinking, the perfect length of time to demonstrate that the Lord was superior to all the gods of Egypt (Exodus 7:14-25).

The second plague: Frogs coming from the Nile River

This plague intended to prove that the Egyptian goddess “Heqt” is not god. This

Egyptian goddess, a form of a frog was regarded as the god of resurrection who assisted women in child birth. The frogs came up from the rivers and were in their houses, in their food, in their clothing, in every place possible. From the greatest to the least, no one in Egypt escaped the plague of frogs. Pharaoh’s magicians were able to bring more frogs in their attempt to imitate the power of God, but only Moses was able to make the frogs go away (Exodus 8:1-15).

The third plague: Gnats

This plague intended to prove that the Egyptian god “Geb”, god of the earth is not god. It was believed that the Egyptian god “Geb” was over the dust of the earth. At the command of the Lord to Moses, Aaron was told to stretch forth his rod and smite the dust of the earth. When he did the dust became gnats throughout the land, on both people and beasts. The very dust that was referred to in the creation process of man is now used to plague men, as a reminder of his mortality and sin which both lead to death. Finally, the magicians of Pharaoh are humiliated, being unable to compete with this power. They said: “This is the finger of God” This was the last plague that required Aaron’s involvement, as the next set of three plagues are issued by the word of Moses himself (Exodus 8:16-19).
