Exercise 1.2

Give a short view of the presence of God in Africa before the advent of Christianity and Islam.

Redemption from slavery

The book of Exodus is about God hearing Israel’s cries for help, rescuing them from their oppressors, and making them His own people. He saved them out of bondage in Egypt. The redemption of Israel from slavery in Egypt is a fore shadow of the redemption of humanity from sin through Christ.

The Sinai Covenant

The book of Exodus expresses the covenant of God with Israel at Sinai. At Sinai

God made a solemn and binding agreement with the people of Israel, establishing

Himself as their God and Israelites as His people. Nonetheless, the covenant came with conditions and benefits for the Israelites if they uphold the agreement.

The concept of God’s love and compassion

The book of Exodus presents God as loving and compassionate. In Exodus 34:6-7,

God describes Himself as “merciful and gracious, slow to anger, and abounding in steadfast love and faithfulness.” On one hand, God’s love implies His grace, kindness, mercy, and tenderness entangled with each other. On the other hand, God’s compassion is the highest form of love related to His mercy, kindness, patience, forgiveness and love interwoven. In the book of Exodus, God blessed the sons of

Israel who came to Egypt as a small family of Jacob but they eventually increased in number and became prosperous. When God’s people experienced slavery and oppression under the Egyptian captivity, He defended and rescued them from the hands of Pharaoh. From Egypt God led His people through the desert. At Sinai God gave the people of Israel His expectations, that is to say, He handed to them a list of instructions outlining what to do and what were prohibited to them for the sake of their common good. All these are expressions of God’s love and compassion for

His people.
