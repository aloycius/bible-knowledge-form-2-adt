There are several characteristics that define Moses as an extraordinary religious leader. From the moment of his encounter with the Lord God through the unconsumed burning bush, Moses became a charismatic type of a leader, courageous, faithful to his Master (God), and executed all that the Lord commanded him to do. Moses was physically strong, energetic, not troubled with tough works. Always merciful and compassionate about others, he pleaded for Israelites’ forgiveness when they went astray. Always humble before the Lord and before his people, Moses was always willing to accept the advice from his father in-law Jethro and from other people too.

Beside these positive attributes of Moses’ leadership, it must equally be said that, as a human being, he made mistakes. This is especially seen when he struck the rock twice, instead of ordering it to bring forth water as instructed by God (Numbers

20:1-13).

Lessons from Moses’ leadership for modern leaders

There are dozens of leadership insights to be leant by modern leaders from Moses.

Modern leaders must know that all powers and the rights to leadership come from

God. That those who are aspiring to be servant leaders must be God-fearing if they are to lead according to God’s plan. To safeguard the welfare of the people, a leader must be courageous enough to face challenges and dominant hierarchies on behalf of his/her people. That pursuing public development and the common good of the people, a leader must be dedicated, compassionate, with necessary bravery require to overcome the obstacles that are getting in the way of the projected development.

Moreover, one of the greatest lessons from Moses’ leadership is that a leader should not despair, nor become obsessed with the successes of leadership. Instead, a leader must consistently and tirelessly work hard for the success of the people.

Seen from the perspective of Moses, a great leader has to nurture and mentor the next generation of leaders as well as delegate part of his/her power as a means of empowering corporate leadership. And finally, the leader must never be corrupt nor allow himself or herself to be corrupted or addicted by power.
