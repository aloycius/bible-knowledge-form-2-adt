Statistics, Census and Resource Management in Ancient Israel

From their nature in the book of Numbers, statistics and census intended to identify how much human resources the community had and what consumptions needed for such community. In Numbers, the census was conducted not only for military purposes but also for the natural and residential resources management and distribution. Drawing upon this perspective, the census conducted in the book of Numbers aimed to lay down the ground for resources management among the people of Israel in the following ways: fair distribution of natural resources and logical investigation and planning.

As the Israelites embarked toward the Promised Land, they were still facing a challenge relating to settlement. So, they needed a fair mechanism for distribution of the land in Canaan. The census conducted was strategically meant to establish the exact number of people in each of the twelve tribes of Israel. Thus, the census enabled distribution of the land and natural resources according to the family size and needs of each tribe, hence, promoting justice, equality, and efficiency.

Planning is an integral part of the census; it is a key aspect in human development.

Planning is about working for tomorrow while living today. With this understanding in the book of Numbers, the Israelites were obliged to plan how the Promised Land and its limited resources were to be distributed when the people finally entered the land. In order to plan well or accordingly, one needs a thorough investigation to obtain a detailed data and a clear mapping plan that is capable of facilitating demographic movement and encampment of a large community manageable.

Apart from demographic issues, the book of Numbers provides liturgical data management. This implies that records pertaining to the offerings and gifts offered by the Israelites were all kept in the books. Numbers chapter seven provides a detailed account of the offerings that each tribe offered for the dedication of the altar.

Each offering was precisely listed, including the weight of silver platters, bowls, and gold dishes. According to Numbers chapter 7:12-17, the offerings from the tribe of
