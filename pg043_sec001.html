The Commandment “You shall not commit adultery”

Adultery is an act of performing sexual intercourse outside the marriage bond.

Sexual intercourse was ordained in the garden of Eden and made sacred by God only to be used for procreation and intimacy among the married couple; starting with

Adam and Eve. Thus, the Commandment urges the married couples to be faithful to their marriage bonds as it can be seen in Exodus 20:14. However, adultery should not be confused with fornication. Fornication refers to a sexual intercourse between two unmarried people. Biblically, fornication is both sin and morally unacceptable behaviour in the society.

Furthermore, committing adultery and fornication can lead to pregnancies and early marriages among girls. Sometimes, adultery may lead to students’ dismissal or drop out from school. From a social point of view, this Commandment is not only advocating for faithfulness and uprightness but also prohibits adultery and fornication as a means preventing the spread of sexual transmitted diseases such as

HIV/AIDS and other diseases transmitted through sexual contact such as gonorrhoea and syphilis.

Sinful sexual practices

Apart from adultery and fornication, the commandment “You shall not commit adultery”, equally advocates for the deactivation of other sinful sexual practices of: incest – which refers to an act of conducting sex among blood relatives; bestiality

– referring to a sexual action involving human beings with animals; masturbation-refers to an action whereby an individual stimulates his/her genitals for sexual pleasure; homo sexuality – broadly refers to an act of sexual relationship among people of the same sex; last but not least is rape – basically referring to all forms of forced sexual contacts. Together with these unlawful sexual practices, this commandment extends its moral ban to all forms of unlawful accommodation of sexual sensitivity such as watching pornographic materials, visiting ‘nude areas’, and dressing immodestly for both boys and girls.
