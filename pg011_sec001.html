The concept of the book of Exodus

The book of Exodus is one of the five books of the Pentateuch. It is the second book in the canonical arrangement of the books of the Bible. In it, we find the stories of the life of Hebrews in Egypt: prosperity and oppression, the birth, call, and mission of Moses. There is also a story of the Ten Plagues and the first Passover. In addition, the book continues recounting the departure of God’s children to the Promised Land, the parting of the Red Sea, the covenant at Sinai, the Ten Commandments and the story of the apostasy.

The name and authorship of the book

The name “Exodus” comes from the Greek word “ἔξοδος” (exodos), which translates to “going out” or “departure.” The book gets its name from the event of the Israelites’ departure from Egypt to the Promised Land (Exodus 12:29-15:21). The Hebrew name for the book is “Shemot” which means names, referring to the list of the names of the sons of Israel who went down into Egypt with Jacob. Traditionally, the book is ascribed to Moses as the author. The book consists of 40 chapters.
