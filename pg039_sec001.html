God’s plan and destiny for Israel

God’s redemptive plan for Israel was a conditional and subject to loyalty. To ensure the redemption is to happen, a covenant was set between God and the people of

Israel. The covenant was not based on the law rather it was intended to ensure the

Israelites about God’s initial plan for their salvation and sustainability as recoded in

Exodus 19:6. Drawing on these evidences, it is undeniably true that God intended for Israel to be a valuable treasure. He wanted them to be a people with a unique role in God’s plan for the reconstruction of the whole world, a people of great value and concern to God.

Through the people of Israel, God intended to build a universal kingdom of devoted people who would propagate and uphold God’s instructions in their nations. The whole nation of Israel was to act as mediators of God’s grace to the nations of the world. In short, God’s choice of Israel has a wider ‘universal’ purpose and relevance.

It is this intended social and spiritual responsibility placed upon the people of Israel that is often referred to as a “holy nation” through whom other nations were to experience God’s grace and love (Exodus 24:1-8).

Preparations to encounter God

The people of Israel must have been excited to know that they were about to meet

God. However, there were conditions to be observed and respected in relation to

God’s holy presence at Mount Sinai. That included: peoples’ consecration, washing their garments and being ready for the day the Lord will come down upon Mount

Sinai in the sight of all the people. To comply with the given conditions, Moses consecrated the Israelites and instructed them to wash their garments for the divine encounter, set to take place on the third day. They were instructed to come near the mountain only when the trumpet sounds long (Exodus 19:12-13).

Moses’ instructions were solely intended to set boundaries for the divine encounter with the Lord. By setting the boundaries and announcing death penalty against breaching the stated instructions; God showed Israel that obedience is a paramount concern in a covenantal relationship. The trumpet was to be used as a symbol of invitation for the covenantal gathering at Mount Sinai. The people could only come near God’s presence if they were clean enough and willing to abide to God’s covenant. The blowing of the trumpet signalled the fact that the invitation was open to all people.
