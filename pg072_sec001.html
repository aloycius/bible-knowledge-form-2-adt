Strength and bravery in the book of Joshua

The commission of Joshua to lead the Israelites marked the transition of leadership from Moses to Joshua. This transition is significant not just for its historical context but also for its enduring lessons on faith, responsibility, and divine assurance (Joshua

1:6-9).

Strength and bravery are clear in the book of Joshua as demonstrated in life and mission of Joshua, offering insights into overcoming challenges through steadfast belief and obedience to God. God’s directives to Joshua to embody strength and bravery are not merely an encouragement but also a command that abridges the essence of his mission. This instruction is repeated several times, underscoring its importance. The repetition serves not just as a reminder but as a reinforcement of the mental and emotional fortitude required to lead a nation on the edge of enormous change.

The word ‘strength’ as used in the Bible, has nothing to do with our physical strength but rather a spiritual strength which is given by God to us when we are weak and delicate. The spiritual strength is manifested in believers as they stand strong against the forces of deconstruction. Therefore, strength is a gift of the Lord that gives us an internal fortitude that enables us to stand firm for the sake of God and defending

His truth. As Prophet Isaiah declared, “The Lord God is my strength and my song”

(Isiah 12:2). Apostle Peter reminds us, “After you have suffered a little while, the

God of all grace, who has called you to his eternal glory in Christ, will himself restore, confirm, strengthen, and establish you” (1 Peter 5:10). Bravery comprises of discipline, awareness, and insight. It calls for the ability to understand your strengths, having insights into what you want, and skillfully maneuvering your world to bring out the best to yourself and to those around you. Indeed, bravery is the state of having or showing mental or moral strength to face danger, fear or difficulty.

Bravery can be physical like rescuing someone from danger, or moral, like standing up for your idea in the face of opposition. In a biblical sense, bravery isn’t just about maneuvering your world, but trusting in God’s power and promises, even when facing impossible challenges, as demonstrated by the biblical figures like Moses,

Joshua, and Jesus. The Bible emphasises that true bravery stems from recognizing

God’s presence and sustaining strengths, rather than relying on one’s own abilities.
