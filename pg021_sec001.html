Excuse due to lack of proof

Moses’ third response to the God’s call was that he lacks concrete proof that he has been called by God. “And Moses answered and said, ‘Behold they will not believe me, nor hearken to my voice, for they will say, “The Lord has not appeared to you”

(Exodus 4:1). We can see again how the previous years of Moses’ experience in the land of Egypt had affected him. The people of Israel had not hearkened to him forty years earlier. Why would they hearken to him now? In response to this, the Lord gave Moses three signs: (1) The rod turning into a serpent. (2) The hand becoming leprous and (3) Water turning into blood.

The first sign was of the rod turning into a serpent. The Lord asked Moses to look at what he was holding. Moses saw that it was his familiar rod with which he had daily tended the sheep. God told Moses to cast the rod down and to step back. Moses obeyed the Lord, and the rod turned into a serpent. Moses was startled at the sight of the serpent, but the Lord told him to pick it up. Moses knew from his Egyptian background that the only way to pick up a serpent was by the head, so that it could not bite him. However, the Lord told Moses to pick it up by the tail. In the very act of picking up the serpent by the tail instead of the head, Moses was learning obedience to God’s commands in contrast to the ways of Egypt. Moses was learning that it was not going to be by Egyptian reasoning or logic that he would deliver Israel, but by

God’s Word and Spirit. The fear and unbelief that Moses showed was answered by

God through his miraculous experience with the rod. The other two signs of leprosy and water turning into blood also showed Moses that the most common things in the hands of man become opportunities for the Lord to show forth His power when they are submitted to His command. In a similar way, God teaches every one of His leaders that His Spirit is the key to ministry’s success.

Excuse of lack of eloquence

Moses’ fourth response to the call of the Lord was that he was unable to speak

(Exodus 4:10-17). “And Moses said to the Lord, “I am not eloquent…. I am slow of speech, and of a slow tongue.” This inability to speak is quite a contrast to Moses of forty years earlier who was a man “mighty in words.” Moses had lost all confidence in himself as a spokesman. In response to this, the Lord appointed Aaron the brother to Moses as a spokesperson.
