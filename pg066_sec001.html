The conquest of Jericho is recorded in Joshua 5:13-6:27. The narrative of the conquest of Jericho begins before the Israelites crossed the Jordan River. In order to avoid repeated failures, Joshua tasked two men who entered Jericho to spy the city. They arrived at Rahab’s house who was a harlot and spent the night. The king of Jericho, heard the news about the presence of the two Israelites who entered the city, and demanded that Rahab must reveal the whereabouts of the spies. She told him that she was unaware of their whereabouts. The next morning, Rahab professes her faith in God to the men and acknowledges her belief that Canaan was divinely reserved for the Israelites from the beginning (Joshua 2:1-7).

The capture of Jericho began with a theophany, that is, the revelation of the Lord.

Joshua saw a man standing in front of him holding a sword, the commander of the

Lord’s army. This was a sign to Joshua that the conquest would be God’s personal undertaking and not through a militarised operation. Thus, the Lord gave Joshua the following instructions: (1) Joshua and the soldiers had to march around the city once a day for six days with seven priests carrying ram’s horns ahead of the Ark. (2)

On the seventh day they had to march seven times while the priests were blowing the horns (trumpets). (3) At the seventh round the priests had to blow loudly with a long blast, and the people would shout loudly and by doing so, the city walls would collapse. Then, the whole army would go straight into the city.

Acting on instructions from the Lord, the Israelites marched around Jericho for seven days and conquered the city, when its walls miraculously fell down. Israel destroyed the city and everything in it, except for Rahab and her family. From the capture of Jericho one can deduce the following lessons: (1) God is good and fights for His people. (2) Being faithful to God’s commandments merits God’s favour.
