Themes of the book of Exodus

A theme in a book implies a vital message, lesson or the teaching that the author expected the audience to draw. The themes of the book of Exodus imply the teachings that can be deduced from the book of Exodus. The book of Exodus is characterized by a number of themes. The following are some of the themes inherent in the book of Exodus.

God is One

This theme is clearly stated in the book of Exodus. Through the ten plagues, the

Lord God showed that there is no any other God except the God of Israel. Moreover, through the commandments, God Himself testified the truth that He is One. The belief on the existence of one God is called monotheism which is opposite of polytheism. Polytheism refers to the belief in the existence of many gods as the

Egyptians believed. The Monotheistic God controls nature and uses nature to fulfil

His divine plans. He is merciful, loving, protector, and the ultimate provider of the needs of His people.

God is mighty and powerful

Another theme which is prominent in the book of Exodus is the fact that God is mighty and powerful. “Mighty” implies God’s ability to act powerfully and decisively to reign over all situations and conditions. God is powerful refers to God’s sovereignty and control over all things both in the natural and supernatural world, and in the affairs of humanity. This theme is clear in the context of Exodus as it is manifested through the manner in which God delivered His people from Egypt.

God is a liberator

Throughout the Bible, God revealed Himself as the creator and liberator. The concept of God as a liberator is seen in relation to how He liberated or redeemed His people from slavery in Egypt.
